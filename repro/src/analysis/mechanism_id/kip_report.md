# KIP (Key-Inversion Parity) -- generation, sanity, audit and reveal ladder

Generated by `src/analysis/mechanism_id/scripts/kip_make_report.py`. Every
number below is read from `kip_audit.csv` and `kip_oracle_reveal.csv`.


## Task

Each sequence contains every one of the `m` hidden key letters exactly once,
at uniformly random positions; the remaining positions are filled i.i.d.
uniform from the distractor alphabet `A \ S`, so a key letter never repeats.
Reading the key letters in sequence order and mapping through `kappa` gives a
permutation `pi` of `{1..m}`, and

    Y = 1  iff  inv(pi) is even         (equivalently, iff pi is an even permutation)

`rho = 0.5` and `AUC* = 1.000` hold by construction, since `sign(pi)` is uniform
for `m >= 2`. Sizes (400K/50K/50K), the 80/10/10 split and seed handling match
Tricky Deterministic (tag `6`); no class-balancing subsample is needed.


| tag | m | n | S in kappa order | rule seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `kip_m4` | 4 | 20 | C < O < T < A | 959697 |
| `kip_m6` | 6 | 20 | G < H < L < Y < I < Z | 959699 |


The hidden rule is **sampled per dataset**, not hardcoded, and stored beside
the CSVs as `<TAG>_rule.json`. This is a deliberate departure from the other
variants: it is what prevents the code/data key-set drift documented in
`report.md` (paper says `S = {W,D,Q,J,X,U}`, the parity generator used `N`).

## Oracle and reveal ladder

Because `inv(pi) = C(m,2) - sum(precedence bits)`, the pairwise precedence
bits determine the label exactly, which gives a closed-form oracle needing no
training. The ladder then asks what a 64-unit MLP needs to be handed.


| rung | model | dim | m=4 AUC | m=6 AUC | expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `oracle_closed_form` | closed_form | 6 / 15 | 1.000000 | 1.000000 | 1.0 exactly |
| `a_raw_tokens` | logreg | 520 | 0.5020 | 0.5022 | chance |
| `a_raw_tokens` | mlp_64 | 520 | 0.4989 | 0.4996 | chance to partial (must discover S and kappa) |

## Findings

- **m=4: the strongest baseline in the ladder is `max_over_lag` / xgboost at AUC 1.0000.**
- **m=6: the strongest baseline in the ladder is `kgram_k4` / kgram_estimator at AUC 0.5033.**
- Fixed-lag pair counts, the family that saturates the compliance tasks, span AUC 0.4974-0.5238 across every lag and both m. That shortcut is closed.
- m=4, lag-agnostic all-pairs families (these reconstruct the full precedence matrix, so they *determine* Y): linear 0.5002-0.5010, nonlinear 0.5903-1.0000.
- m=6, lag-agnostic all-pairs families (these reconstruct the full precedence matrix, so they *determine* Y): linear 0.4997-0.5022, nonlinear 0.5012-0.5027.
- Closed-form oracle from precedence bits: AUC = 1.000000 exactly for every m.
- Negative control (key-position membership bits): at chance for every m and model, as required (range 0.4986-0.5004).
- m=4: the rank sequence determines the precedence bits, yet the same MLP scores 0.5077 on the rank sequence against 1.0000 on the precedence bits -- identical information, different representation.
- m=6: the rank sequence determines the precedence bits, yet the same MLP scores 0.5003 on the rank sequence against 1.0000 on the precedence bits -- identical information, different representation.
